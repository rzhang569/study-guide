## Random variables, expectation and variance

::: terms
- random variable: a number determined by the outcome, e.g. the number of heads
- PMF $p(x)$: probability mass function: $P(X = x)$ for each value
- expectation $E[X]$: probability-weighted average of the values; the long-run average
- variance $\mathrm{Var}(X)$: expected squared distance from the mean; how spread out X is
- Bernoulli(p): 1 with probability p, 0 otherwise
- Binomial(n, p): number of successes in n independent Bernoulli(p) trials
:::

### 3.1 Expectation {#l3-exp p3}
src: L3 p.5–9
lead: Multiply each value by its probability and add.

$$E[X]=\sum_x x\,p(x)$$

::: eg
A fair die: (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6) / 6 = 3.5 (L3 p.6). No roll ever shows 3.5; it is the average over many rolls.
:::

::: trap
Weight by probability. A game that pays $10 with probability 0.2 and costs $3 otherwise is worth 0.2 · 10 − 0.8 · 3 = −$0.40 per play, not the plain average of $10 and −$3.
:::

### 3.2 Linearity of expectation {#l3-lin p3}
src: L3 p.11–15
lead: $E[aX + bY + c] = aE[X] + bE[Y] + c$, always, even when X and Y depend on each other.

::: eg
Four people get their hats back in random order. Person i gets their own hat with probability 1/4, so the expected number of matches is 4 · 1/4 = 1, even though the matches are dependent (L3 p.14).
:::

::: plain
Break a messy count into a sum of yes/no indicators, take the expectation of each, add. You never need the joint distribution.
:::

::: trap
Linearity is about sums. $E[XY] = E[X]E[Y]$ needs independence, and $E[g(X)] \ne g(E[X])$ in general (e.g. $E[X^2] \ne (E[X])^2$).
:::

### 3.3 Variance and the binomial {#l3-var p2}
src: L3 p.18–28
lead: Compute variance as $E[X^2] - (E[X])^2$; shifting X does not change it, scaling by a multiplies it by $a^2$.

$$\mathrm{Var}(X)=E[X^2]-(E[X])^2,\qquad \mathrm{Var}(aX+b)=a^2\,\mathrm{Var}(X)$$
$$X\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,p):\quad E[X]=np,\qquad \mathrm{Var}(X)=np(1-p)$$

::: figure bars
title: P(X = k), X ~ Bin(10, 0.5)
x: 0..10
y: comb(10, x) * 0.5^10
hl: 3..7
mark: 5 | mean np = 5
caption: Ten fair coin flips. The bars for 3–7 heads (dark) hold about 89% of the probability; the spread is $\sqrt{np(1-p)} \approx 1.58$ (L3 p.24).
:::

::: trap
$\mathrm{Var}(3X - 2) = 9\,\mathrm{Var}(X)$: square the 3, drop the −2. Writing 3 Var(X) or subtracting 2 are the two classic slips.
:::

::: more Why Var(X) = E[X²] − (E[X])² (not on the exam)
Let $\mu = E[X]$. Expand $(X - \mu)^2 = X^2 - 2\mu X + \mu^2$ and take expectations with linearity: $E[X^2] - 2\mu^2 + \mu^2 = E[X^2] - \mu^2$.
:::
